# MiMo-V2.6-Flash-MOPD vs DeepSeek V4 0731 / V4.1 Flash — serving on one 8×H100 node

Study `mimo-v26` ([plan](../../docs/mimo-v2.6-plan.md), [architecture and inference walkthrough](../../docs/mimo-v2.6-inference.md)). `XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Flash-MOPD` @ `2479e2d0029eca9a34cc7e7f55a121925f81908e`, measured 2026-09-28. It uses the same node, vLLM build (`0.30.1rc1.dev223+g44af287eb`), server limits (TP 8 + EP, 0.90 memory, `max_model_len` 262,144, `max_num_seqs` 64, chunked prefill 8,192), harness, seeds, and workload definitions as the DeepSeek study in [its report](../v41-vs-0731/report.md). DeepSeek numbers below are from that study and were not re-run. Tokenizers differ: random-token prompts were regenerated per model to the same token counts, and chat templates add 4–9 tokens. Ratios are **MiMo / reference** (> 1 is better for throughput, worse for latency). Values are **mean ± SD over 3 repeats**. The inconclusive rule and evidence labels match the DeepSeek report.

**Validity.** Speculation off: 90 of 90 runs valid (concurrency 24, isolated 27, context 12, prefix cache-off 9, prefix cold/prewarmed 18). Speculative arms: MTP 12/12, DFlash 12/12 (114/114 valid in total). One harness crash before any request was sent is listed under [Failures](#failures-and-deviations). Thinking is off via `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking=false` (the template emits `<think></think>`). The 4-prompt smoke test answered 4/4 correctly with no reasoning text.

## Key insights

1. **MiMo is the fastest of the three on every uncached workload:** 1.27–1.42× 0731 and 1.43–1.73× V4.1. It prefills at 33–34 µs per prompt token from 8K to 64K (0731: 41.6, V4.1: 54.5) and decodes at 5.5–6.3 ms per token (DeepSeek: 7.4–7.9). *Measured.*
2. **The advantage is architectural and removes work rather than FLOPs.** MiMo touches about 16% *more* linear weights per token than 0731 (≈ 14.2B vs 12.2B, estimated from shapes). It is faster because it has none of DeepSeek's per-layer attention machinery (sparse attention, learned indexer, KV compressor: −51 ms per 8K chunk), lighter projections with no shared expert (−29 ms), and plain residual norms instead of 4-stream mHC (−19 ms). It gives back 34 ms on 8 active experts and more all-reduces. *Trace.*
3. **Long context favours MiMo, contrary to the pre-run prediction.** Its 9 full-attention layers grow from 9 to 39 ms per chunk between 16K and 57K context, but 0731's attention plus indexer grows from 51 to 94 ms. MiMo's lead over 0731 widens from 1.28× (16K) to 1.42× (260K) at c8. *Measured + trace.*
4. **All-reduce is now the dominant cost:** 44% of a MiMo prefill chunk and 27% of a decode step, at about 1.0 ms per 67 MB call (≈ 67 GB/s). This is the next optimization target. The model card's DP-attention deployment is the natural test. *Trace; remedy is a hypothesis.*
5. **Prefix caching needs a second touch, like 0731.** A new 64K prefix is reusable only after two requests have computed it, identical hit counts to 0731 in every run. MiMo still beats 0731 by 1.3× in every cache state, and beats V4.1 except with **many distinct prefixes warmed once**, where V4.1's one-touch reuse wins 1.72× (16 prefixes, prewarmed). *Measured.*
6. **Native speculation helps at low load and holds a small gain at high load.** DFlash (7 tokens) gives **1.68×** at c1 and MTP gives 1.53×. Both give 1.15–1.16× at c4, 1.06× at c16, and 1.01–1.02× at c64. That is slightly better at load than DSpark was on DeepSeek (0.98–1.04×), still limited by prefill. This build runs only MTP layer 0, reused for 3 steps (position-3 acceptance 0.17). *Measured + source.*

## 2. Why MiMo is faster: components (rank-0 traces, one 8,192-token prefill chunk)

Traces `prefill16k_decode8` and `prefill64k_decode4` on the idle-profiler launch. Per-step breakdowns are in `data/mimo-v26/profiles/`. [bench/trace_breakdown.py](../../bench/trace_breakdown.py) now also files FA3's `FlashAttnFwdSm90` under attention; the DeepSeek breakdowns are byte-identical after this change.

| Component | MiMo 16K chunk 2 | 0731 16K chunk 2 | V4.1 16K chunk 2 | MiMo − 0731 | MiMo 57K ctx | 0731 57K ctx |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Kernel sum** | **216.3** | **293.1** | **399.2** | **−76.8** | **244.7** | **338.3** |
| TP all-reduce | 94.8 | 79.4 | 107.7 | +15.4 | 93.3 | 78.7 |
| MoE expert GEMM (Marlin) | 71.6 | 53.3 | 73.0 | +18.3 | 71.8 | 52.6 |
| MoE routing / combine | 4.6 | 7.3 | 7.9 | −2.7 | 4.6 | 7.3 |
| Attention | 9.1 | 41.9 | 49.5 | −32.8 | 38.8 | 53.2 |
| Indexer / top-k | — | 8.9 | 6.1 | −8.9 | — | 40.5 |
| QKV norm / RoPE / KV insert | 1.1 | 10.2 | 9.6 | −9.1 | 1.1 | 10.2 |
| Dense GEMM | 11.2 | 39.7 | 91.5 | −28.5 | 11.2 | 39.8 |
| Norm / residual (DeepSeek: mHC) | 8.5 | 27.9 | 32.1 | −19.4 | 8.5 | 27.9 |
| Other elementwise (MiMo: SwiGLU 11.8) | 15.5 | 24.5 | 19.1 | −9.0 | 15.5 | 28.2 |
| Layers / all-reduce calls | 48 / 95 | 43 / 87 | 40 / 81 | | | |

- **Dense GEMM uses the fast path, as predicted.** MiMo's 128×128-block FP8 layers resolve to `FlashInferFp8DeepGEMMDynamicBlockScaledKernel` (0731's path), not Marlin (V4.1). Its projections are also smaller, and there is no shared expert.
- **All-reduce uses symm-mem multimem, as predicted** (67 MB messages, the same size as 0731). MiMo makes more calls (2 per layer × 48), so all-reduce is 44% of its chunk time.
- **Attention:** 39 sliding-window layers cost 1.6 ms in total. The 9 full FA3 layers cost 7.5 ms at 16K and 37 ms at 57K context. That is cheaper than DeepSeek's sparse attention plus indexer at every measured context, because DeepSeek's indexer also grows with context.
- **Why the FLOP-based long-context prediction failed:** the linear layers run at about 105 TFLOP/s per GPU (≈ 28 GFLOP/token in 33.5 µs across 8 GPUs), far below peak, while FA3 runs near peak. So attention's share of *time* is much smaller than its share of FLOPs.

**Decode step (c1, 16K):** MiMo 5.67 ms span with 880 kernels, vs 0731 7.77 ms with 1,834 kernels. Dense GEMM is 0.93 vs 3.48 ms (q-LoRA with 64 × 512-wide heads, grouped o-LoRA, and the shared expert on 0731). MoE is 1.34 vs 1.39 ms (8 vs 6 experts, but MXFP4 halves weight bytes). All-reduce is 1.54 vs 1.34 ms, attention 0.60 vs 0.99 ms, and mHC 0 vs 0.89 ms. Unlike V4.1, both MiMo and 0731 decode steps are attributable in the trace.

## 3. Native speculative decoding

Arms per [docs/mimo-v2.6-plan.md](../../docs/mimo-v2.6-plan.md), concurrency c1/4/16/64 × 3, prefix caching off, each vs MiMo's own speculation-off run. Tables: [spec_tables.md](spec_tables.md) (`bench/spec_compare.py --preset mimo`).

| Conc. | Off | MTP k3 (layer 0 ×3) | DFlash k7 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 130.1 | 199.1 ± 3.0 (**1.53×**) | 219.2 ± 5.3 (**1.68×**) |
| 4 | 303.6 | 350.3 ± 8.8 (**1.15×**) | 350.9 ± 12.8 (**1.16×**) |
| 16 | 432.0 | 457.7 ± 0.9 (**1.06×**) | 456.1 ± 4.9 (**1.06×**) |
| 64 | 504.6 | 516.6 ± 0.5 (**1.02×**) | 512.2 ± 0.3 (**1.01×**) |

- **MTP as run by this build:** vLLM's `mimo_v2_mtp.py` hardcodes `num_mtp_layers = 1`, so it loads only `mtp.layers.0` and applies it recursively for 3 steps. It logs *"Enabling num_speculative_tokens > 1 will run multiple times of forward on same MTP layer."* The checkpoint's layers 1–2 are unused. Per-position acceptance is 0.78 / 0.39 / 0.17, and 2.33–2.35 tokens are committed per round (45% of proposed). This is **not** the model's intended 3-layer MTP; the model card's SGLang recipe uses multi-layer EAGLE.
- **MTP still gains at c16/c64**, unlike DSpark on DeepSeek. A plausible reason is that one MTP layer is a cheap drafter relative to MiMo's step. This is a hypothesis: no speculative trace was taken.
- **DFlash:** the `dflash/` drafter (qwen3-type, 5 SWA layers, block 8, taps target layers 0/11/23/35/47) loaded as `DFlashDraftModel` with 7 speculative tokens and its own CUDA graphs. Acceptance is 21–25% of proposed tokens and 2.48–2.73 tokens committed per round. Per-position acceptance is 0.64 / 0.40 / 0.24 / 0.13 / 0.09 / 0.06 / 0.05 at c64, so positions 5–7 add little. Its c1 TPOT is 2.4 ms (MTP 2.9, off 5.6).
- **Latency trade at c64:** TPOT falls to 81.8 ms (DFlash) and 99.0 ms (MTP) from 116.9 ms, while TTFT p50 rises (DFlash c64 repeat 1: 9.4 s vs 2.3 s off). This is the same closed-loop effect as DSpark in [the DeepSeek report §4c](../v41-vs-0731/report.md#4c-why-the-gain-disappears-and-where-the-latency-goes-c64): throughput is prefill-bound, so faster decode moves waiting time into the prefill queue.
- **KV capacity:** off 6,906,032 → MTP 6,749,906 → DFlash 6,086,967 tokens.
- **Output agreement with speculation off (greedy):** exact matches are 0/32 (c1), 0/48 (c4), 2–3/192 (c16), and 4–7/768 (c64) ([spec_output_match.md](spec_output_match.md)). As in the DeepSeek study, there is no same-seed determinism control, so this is not evidence of lossy decoding. c1 covers repeats 2–3 only: speculation-off repeat 1 is `repeat-1-rerun1` after the harness crash.
- **Recommendation:** for low-concurrency interactive serving, DFlash (1.68× at c1). At c ≥ 16 on long prompts, either drafter gives ≤ 6%.

## Not covered (follow-ups)

All-reduce / DP-attention arm (biggest lever); faithful 3-layer MTP (another runtime or a vLLM change); MTP k1; decode-bound speculative workload (16K/2,048); real-text acceptance; two-touch prewarm arm; TP 4 deployment; real-text quality.
